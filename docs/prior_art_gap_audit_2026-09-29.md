# Prior-art boundary and next experiment, September 29, 2026

## Decision

Do not pitch “a judge monitors chain-of-thought and decides whether to verify, repair, branch, trim, or stop” as a new method. Direct precedents already implement test-time process verification, rollback/feedback, state-dependent verifier allocation, same-prefix branching, and action-conditioned intervention-value estimation. **The core estimand and data-collection protocol we previously proposed are already directly present in prior work. We have not validated a methods-level novelty gap.** A possible remaining study is only an empirical comparison of Jev as a particular judgment source against cheap/local features inside an established intervention-control design. This extension is not yet a defensible novelty claim, and its Jev-dependent form is on hold pending contract clarification.

## September 29, 06:16 EDT — direct Jev-control literature update

A focused search of arXiv and public code indexes surfaced an additional close paper published September 22, *REFLEX with Jev for Efficient Selective Control in LLM Agents* ([arXiv:2609.26532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26532)). I inspected the paper's full HTML methods, experiments and discussion (§§3–6 and appendices B–D). REFLEX puts pinned Jev inside an agent trajectory as a typed bounded-action selector, runs high-confidence decisions directly, and sends low-confidence or generative decisions to a fallback model. It compares strong-only, a cheap Flash-only/cascade and Jev-based selective control; it also tests fallback-family changes and decision complexity. The paper reports that a cheap generative cascade is competitive on its held-out multi-turn evaluation and limits its claims accordingly. Its reported task success/cost numbers are external author results, not reproduced here.

This **preempts broad claims** that Jev is the first real-time agent supervisor, that a typed Jev decision layer inside a trajectory is new, or that confidence-gated escalation is a new controller. The paper does not evaluate natural-language reasoning-prefix interventions that rewind a CoT segment or branch candidate continuations and score them by independent terminal task outcomes. That distinction is a narrower *experimental setting*, not a methods novelty result: *Calibration Is Not Control* already supplies same-prefix action-conditioned value learning, and *CausalFlow* covers counterfactual repair.

Two other direct Jev papers clarify the remaining boundary. *JEV-as-a-Judge: Accept When Confident, Escalate When Unsure* ([arXiv:2609.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)) evaluates Jev among judge models and frozen confidence cascades, not a generator policy controlled by Jev. *Jev vs. LLMs as Rubric Judges: Cheaper, Faster, and Wrong in the Same Places* ([arXiv:2609.29769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29769)) evaluates 5,003 rubric pairs over nine panels and reports correlated judge errors; it does not execute alternative reasoning interventions or observe their downstream value. These papers reinforce why judge-label agreement, confidence, and price cannot stand in for intervention utility. *Jev-Mem* ([arXiv:2609.23986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23986)) separately preempts broad claims about first use of Jev for adaptive retrieval budgets or stopping inside an agent system.

**Updated claim boundary:** any future contribution must be stated as a source-specific, task-specific empirical result: whether a fixed Jev typed signal changes decisions on inspectable reasoning prefixes enough to improve independently verified downstream outcomes over the same strong cheap/local and compatible published controls, after query, latency, and intervention costs. The current small data do not answer this; action replication showed no reliable benefit for its tested repairs/branch selector, and the arithmetic opportunity gate failed. Before further inference, the exact task/action setting must show enough natural intervention opportunity and a defensible held-out transfer target, and the TypeSafe contract question must be resolved. No new experiment or Jev request was made in this update.
