\begin{abstract}
We evaluate whether TypeSafe Jev's typed semantic judgments add decision value relative to cheaper and local signals when selecting interventions on observable reasoning traces. Small, independently audited studies on procedural graph and arithmetic tasks have not established Jev's incremental value or reliable benefit from the tested actions; two arithmetic-opportunity screens failed a preregistered feasibility gate. Prior work already defines intervention advantage, same-prefix branching, action-conditioned controllers, step-level counterfactual repair, verifier-guided rollback, and counterfactual omission of workflow components. In particular, recent LW2S studies calibrated action-specific skip-safety models and domain guards across math, QA, and code workflows. We therefore make no methods-level novelty claim. The only remaining candidate contribution is a carefully controlled evaluation of this particular judgment source against a strong learned safety controller, including its incremental value, transfer, and full acquisition cost; the service terms and an exact compatible baseline remain unresolved dependencies. This draft reports limits and open dependencies, not positive efficacy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question and prior art}
The question is whether Jev adds value as one *judgment source* within an already studied intervention-control setting. It is not whether a judge agrees with a final verifier. Process-advantage verification relates reasoning steps to subsequent success \cite{pav}; reward-guided speculative decoding controls draft acceptance \cite{rsd}; learned intervention gates, checkpoint branching, and recoverability-aware control already have precedents \cite{calibration,dial,guard}. In particular, \emph{Calibration Is Not Control} defines intervention advantage as the action-conditioned utility difference, introduces same-prefix branching to observe candidate-action outcomes, and evaluates prefix-feature action-conditioned controllers against scalar routing \cite{calibration}. Its estimand and branching protocol directly overlap with the framework we previously proposed; they are not our novelty. \emph{CausalFlow} performs step-level counterfactual replacement and downstream re-execution to find repairs that change task outcomes \cite{causalflow}. More recently, PCCC defines deletion, semantic/evidence substitution, and tool-output perturbation interventions, evaluates terminal verified outcomes under a frozen continuation policy, and uses counterfactual credit for long-horizon agent RL \cite{cvtrl}. This directly preempts the general intervention-value and counterfactual-training parts of our earlier proposal. \emph{interwhen} extracts intermediate states, runs executable/formal verifiers asynchronously, and uses violation feedback to steer or roll back generation \cite{interwhen}; adaptive verifier-call allocation is also studied directly \cite{adaptivecompute}. A robust repair/stop analysis further models noisy verification and repair damage when deciding whether to repeat or stop \cite{vrrstop}. Thus generic real-time judging, action-value estimation, prefix branching, counterfactual repair, verifier-guided training, rollback, or adaptive verification cannot be claimed as new here.

The overlap is concrete in interwhen's Game24 implementation: it periodically spawns a same-model side stream to extract an equation from the visible thinking prefix, uses an executable checker to inject correction feedback or stop when the expression is complete, and then checks the final answer \cite{interwhenCode}. The side stream is extra generation and may itself supply a solution; it must be cost-matched to a no-feedback extraction control. The inspected runner also reuses the monitor's checker for its final score, so our reproduction protocol adds a separate exact AST/Fraction outcome evaluator. These source-level observations do not independently verify interwhen's reported benchmark results. They do establish that our original “judge a reasoning prefix and repair/stop” framing directly overlaps with published code.

The recent \emph{Learning What to Skip} (LW2S) paper is an additional direct comparator for conditional computation. It collects full-workflow and single-component-omission counterfactuals, trains an action-specific logistic safety model, calibrates an abstaining threshold on held-out sources, adds domain-native guards, and permits fall-through to later skip decisions \cite{lw2s}. Its experiments cover MATH, GSM8K, MMLU, and MBPP with Qwen2.5 and Mistral backbones; the authors report 3,290 tasks and 19,740 saved traces across the main, scale-up, and second-topology blocks. The paper reports token savings with aggregate accuracy preserved or improved in most reported settings, while its boundary analyses show shared-error failures. These are author-reported results, not reproduced here. LW2S preempts broad claims for learning when to stop, trim, or omit a verification/summarization component from a reasoning workflow, and it is the strongest published safety-controller family we have identified for our proposed source comparison. It does not evaluate Jev. A viable residual question is therefore narrower: on the same observable prefixes, fixed action, continuation, task split, and full cost ledger, does the typed Jev signal improve held-out terminal outcomes over an independently trained LW2S-style local controller? This remains only a candidate experiment; it needs a task/action contract, an exact released implementation or transparently labeled adaptation, and written TypeSafe-use clarification.

Scientific-discovery evaluation also has mature benchmark and controller precedents. DiscoveryWorld provides 120 simulated challenge instances across eight scientific themes, three difficulty levels, and five supported variations, with separate completion, process, and explanatory-knowledge measures \cite{discoveryworld}. ScienceWorld likewise has published interactive-agent comparisons, including stepwise discriminator guidance (DGAP), learned reward-model planning (ARMAP), and critique-guided iterative improvement (CGI) \cite{dgap,armap,cgi}. These methods do not by themselves answer whether a typed Jev signal adds value at the same observable prefix and fixed action set under fully matched costs, but they establish direct application overlap and make a compatible published-controller comparison necessary for any ScienceWorld study. A pinned-source audit of ARMAP shows that its released ScienceWorld runner sends a completed episode transcript to a learned reward model, applies a task-completion threshold, and may restart with a generated reflection; it does not select among candidate actions from one shared prefix. Its published example pairs a Llama 3.1 8B actor with a VILA1.5-3B reward-model backbone and a task-specific LoRA trained from ScienceWorld preference trajectories. Thus ARMAP may serve as a separate end-to-end comparator, while same-prefix branch scoring would be an adaptation; in either case, train/variation overlap and endpoint alignment must be audited before treating its score as independent evidence \cite{armapcode,armapmodel}. We treat both environments as existing candidates, not contributions of this work. DiscoveryWorld's 120 instances form only 24 theme-by-difficulty strata, so repeated parametric seeds do not justify an unclustered 120-task precision claim; a viable study must first define a natural intervention checkpoint, an independently auditable endpoint, and a transfer design across task families. Our current E1-A work is limited to source-pinned environment, observation-visibility, and replay qualification; it has not yet evaluated an agent.

A separate ScienceWorld Mendelian-classification candidate exposes an additional measurement hazard: its Python API returns the controller observation alongside an \texttt{info} dictionary containing \texttt{variationIdx}, score, and reward metadata \cite{scienceworldAPI}. Because the task variation index seeds the hidden trait mapping, forwarding the full API metadata to a controller would create a direct evaluator-side side channel. The pinned task source's instruction names the phenotype to classify and the two answer boxes, but does not state which answer is correct; that source-level check does not establish what an agent can infer from later observations or interaction. Any future agent input must be constructed from the observation string alone, while keeping metadata evaluator-side. A no-model train/dev-only audit completed on Bouchet (Slurm job 27927761): across 240 training and 120 development variations, all API info records carried the metadata fields while the observation-only controller input contained no \texttt{variationIdx} field; no test variation, label, score value, model, or Jev call was used. The batch exit was 1 because the first remote auditor reserialized JSON before checking the exact-byte protocol hash; the retrieved run passed a corrected local independent audit (18/18 structural checks). This is direct metadata-channel control only, not semantic-leakage, agent-behavior, action-value, or efficacy evidence.

Jev-specific control is also no longer an open-ended novelty claim. \emph{REFLEX with Jev} uses Jev as a typed decision layer inside agent trajectories, executing confident bounded actions and escalating uncertain or generative decisions to fallback models; it compares this controller with strong-only and cheap generative cascades and reports settings where the cascade remains competitive \cite{reflexjev}. \emph{Jev-Mem} uses Jev for adaptive memory routing, retrieval budgets, graph traversal, and stopping \cite{jevmem}. Judge-quality studies compare Jev with other judges and examine confidence-gated cascades, but do not measure the downstream value of executing alternative reasoning interventions \cite{jevjudge,jevjudges}. Together with LW2S, these works require the comparison to include not only cheap or independently trained local features but also a calibrated action-specific safety model with domain guards. Our narrow distinction, if it survives further review, is the *empirical source comparison*: intervene on an inspectable reasoning prefix and measure independently verified terminal outcomes under matched, fully counted costs. This is not a claim of a new control method or a gap established by current evidence.

Even this application boundary must be stated narrowly. Jev has been evaluated for typed semantic choices feeding scientific computations \cite{jevscience}, for selecting lower-level actions under a persistent planner in long-horizon StarCraft II control \cite{jevstar}, and as a behavioral kernel in calibrated population intervention simulations \cite{kite}. These papers preempt broad claims about Jev for science, long-horizon control, or intervention studies. Their targets differ from actions on an LLM's own visible reasoning prefix followed by independent terminal-task verification. This distinction is a candidate empirical niche, not an established novelty gap; the general intervention method has direct prior art. The reported studies are author results and are not reproduced here.

If pursued, our remaining study is a source-comparison question: does a fixed off-the-shelf typed judgment signal add predictive or held-out deployment value beyond strong cheap features, executable checks and a separately qualified local judge, when the action set and same-prefix protocol are held fixed? A recent observational study releases annotations for CLI-agent failures, but its package does not include raw trajectories, so the released labels alone cannot supply controller-visible prefixes \cite{failureprocess}. A transfer or long-horizon extension would need genuinely fresh task families and a predeclared cost model. Even this is an empirical extension, not a validated methods-level gap; a systematic novelty search, an action/task contract that survives the direct overlaps above, and permission to use Jev in this research context are required before making a contribution claim or launching the Jev-dependent study.

\section{Estimand and experimental separation}
Let $H$ be a complete observable checkpoint, $b$ the remaining resource allowance, $a$ an intervention and $\mu$ a frozen subsequent policy. For independently verified terminal success $Y$ and a specified cost $C$, define
\[
Q^\mu(h,b,a)=\mathbb E[Y^{a,\mu}-\lambda C^{a,\mu}\mid H=h,b],\qquad
\Delta^\mu(h,b,a)=Q^\mu(h,b,a)-Q^\mu(h,b,a_0).
\]
Cost units and $\lambda$ must be declared. We currently report success, tokens, service time and dollars separately; there is no measured hardware-independent cost conversion. All-arm experiments clone the same observable checkpoint and run actual continuations. Gold task data is used by the terminal evaluator, never by the intervention selector. Multiple checkpoints or repeats from one problem do not create independent task samples.

We distinguish (i) whether the available actions have useful, reproducible differences; (ii) whether semantic features predict those differences beyond cheap text and token statistics; and (iii) whether the resulting frozen policy improves fresh complete episodes. A failure at one layer does not identify all other layers as ineffective.

\section{Theory, assumptions and scope}
These propositions specialize established experimental identification, decision theory and policy learning. They are not claimed as new general theorems. Their conditions are not certified by a judge's confidence or by the present small experiments. The observable history includes exact tokens and formatting, candidate frontier, tool/environment state, remaining resources, and the fixed model, quantization, sampler and backend. Changing any of these may change the estimand. Random continuations define distributions of potential outcomes, not a uniquely observable individual counterfactual future.

\subsection{Identification and eligibility}
\begin{proposition}[Checkpoint identification]
Suppose interventions and the subsequent policy $\mu$ are well defined; replay restores the checkpoint and environment without cross-arm interference; assignment is independent of potential outcomes conditional on $H$; each studied action has positive assignment probability; and failures/truncations follow a prespecified inclusion rule. Then
\[
Q^\mu(h,a)=\mathbb E[U\mid H=h,A=a].
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consistency gives $U=U^{a,\mu}$ on $A=a$. Conditional randomization implies
$\mathbb E[U^{a,\mu}\mid H=h,A=a]=\mathbb E[U^{a,\mu}\mid H=h]$.
Positivity makes the former conditional mean observable, yielding the result.
\end{proof}
For complete all-arm collection, the mean of $m_{ia}$ replays is conditionally unbiased if its positive replicate count is fixed independently of their outcomes and every replay has the correct marginal continuation distribution. Independence between replays is needed for common elementary variance formulas, not for linearity of expectation. Outcome-dependent stopping can bias the stopped mean. Equal integer seeds after diverging prefixes do not automatically give useful common-random-number coupling.

Let $R$ be a frozen checkpoint-construction protocol and $E$ its pre-intervention eligibility event. The conditional estimand is on $P_R(H\mid E=1)$. If two policies use the same $R$, intervene once and have identical ineligible fallback, then
\[
V_R(\pi)-V_R(\pi_0)=P_R(E=1)\mathbb E[Q_R^\mu(H,\pi(H))-Q_R^\mu(H,\pi_0(H))\mid E=1].
\]
To prove this, decompose the full-episode difference by $E$, cancel the common fallback term and apply the conditional action means. Different prefix builders, eligibility rules or repeated interventions invalidate that derivation. A generate-then-rewind prefix selected using later emitted text is not an online stopping-time checkpoint, even if its retained tokens exactly match a natural prefix. Historical constructed-state experiments must remain separately labeled.

For sparse randomized arms with known $e_a(H)>0$, a frozen policy $\pi$ and independently fitted outcome functions $m_a$, define
\[
\phi_\pi=m_{\pi(H)}(H)+\frac{\mathbf1\{A=\pi(H)\}}{e_A(H)}[U-m_A(H)].
\]
Conditional on $H$ and the fitted functions, the correction has expectation $Q^\mu(H,\pi(H))-m_{\pi(H)}(H)$, so $\mathbb E\phi_\pi=V^\mu(\pi)$. Estimated propensities require additional consistency assumptions; the label ``doubly robust'' alone supplies no guarantee. Cross-fitting must separate original problems.

\subsection{Semantic representation and acquisition}
\begin{proposition}[Free-information comparison]
For a fixed distribution of potential utilities, fixed actions and nested representations $W$ and $(W,J)$, define
\[
V^*_{W,J}=\mathbb E\max_a\mathbb E[U^a\mid W,J],\qquad
V^*_W=\mathbb E\max_a\mathbb E[U^a\mid W].
\]
If acquiring $J$ is free and changes neither state nor budget, $V^*_{W,J}\geq V^*_W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every policy measurable in $W$ is also admissible with $(W,J)$ and can ignore $J$. Taking a supremum over the larger policy class cannot decrease value.
\end{proof}
Finite fitted learners need not improve. If one action is optimal everywhere, informative semantic classifications can have no decision value. If $J=f(H)$, or its randomness is independent of rollout outcomes conditional on $H$, it contributes no external information beyond full history; the hypothesis concerns a computationally useful representation relative to feasible cheap alternatives.

For deterministic known acquisition cost $c_J$, with acquisition affecting subsequent behavior only through information and remaining resource vector $B$, the pre-query comparison is
\[
\mathbb E_{J\mid w,B}\!\left[\max_{a\in\mathcal A(B-c_J)}Q(w,J,B-c_J,a)\right]
\quad\hbox{versus}\quad
\max_{a\in\mathcal A(B)}Q_0(w,B,a).
\]
The maximum belongs inside the expectation over the unobserved signal. Stochastic failure, latency or usage requires integrating over the entire acquisition outcome, including fallback, exhaustion and any changed external state. Hard constraints and cost preferences may coexist, but their units must be declared and fees must not accidentally be subtracted twice.

\subsection{Action-value error and replay precision}
\begin{proposition}[Greedy regret]
At a checkpoint, suppose $\max_a|\widehat Q(a)-Q(a)|\leq\epsilon$. If $a^*\in\arg\max_a Q(a)$ and $\widehat a\in\arg\max_a\widehat Q(a)$, then $Q(a^*)-Q(\widehat a)\leq2\epsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert $\widehat Q(a^*)$ and $\widehat Q(\widehat a)$ between the two true values. The estimated-value difference is nonpositive by selection, while each of the two remaining errors is at most $\epsilon$.
\end{proof}
A unique optimal action with gap exceeding $2\epsilon$ must be selected. Representation and estimation errors add by the triangle inequality when both are bounded uniformly at the relevant states. Neither calibration nor AUROC establishes such a bound.

For $M$ fixed checkpoints, $K$ actions and $m$ independent Bernoulli replays per pair, Hoeffding and a union bound yield
\[
P\!\left(\max_{i,a}|\widehat Q_i(a)-Q_i(a)|>\epsilon\right)
\leq2MK\exp(-2m\epsilon^2).
\]
Thus $m\geq\log(2MK/\delta)/(2\epsilon^2)$ suffices. At $M=48,K=3,\delta=.05,\epsilon=.10$, the rounded requirement is 433 replays per pair. A few repeats can support pooled modeling; they do not supply reliable individual best-action labels. A sample maximum evaluated on its own selection data is optimistic. Independent continuations must assess the selected rule.

\subsection{Sequential control}
Use a finite-horizon history-state process with resource counters, time and acquisition phase included in $H_t$. Absorb early termination with zero future reward and use zero terminal value. Let the deterministic baseline choose $a_{0t}(h)$, with
\[
Q_t^\mu(h,a)=\mathbb E[r_t+V_{t+1}^\mu(H_{t+1})\mid h,a],\quad
A_t^\mu(h,a)=Q_t^\mu(h,a)-V_t^\mu(h).
\]
\begin{proposition}[Conditional sequential improvement]
Suppose both policies share initial distribution, transitions, reward and resource envelope. On every history reachable by $\pi$, assume all errors satisfy
$|\widehat Q_t(h,a)-Q_t^\mu(h,a)|\leq\epsilon_t(h)$.
If $\pi$ deviates only when
\[
\widehat Q_t(h,a)-\widehat Q_t(h,a_{0t})>2\epsilon_t(h),
\]
then $V^\pi\geq V^\mu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a switched state the true difference is at least the estimated difference minus $2\epsilon_t(h)$, hence positive. At an unswitched state it is zero. Since $V_t^\mu(h)=Q_t^\mu(h,a_{0t})$, all visited advantages are nonnegative. Conditional expectation and telescoping give
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}A_t^\mu(H_t,A_t)
=\mathbb E_\pi\sum_t[r_t+V_{t+1}^\mu(H_{t+1})-V_t^\mu(H_t)]
=V^\pi-V^\mu\geq0.
\]
\end{proof}
The uniform premise covers newly reached states, not only baseline prefixes. One-step replay therefore does not establish this result's premises in deployment. A gate evaluated after paying for a judge can only compare policies in that post-query state; query purchase must separately have nonnegative advantage or be included in the full intervention. Judge confidence is not the required simultaneous error bound.

\subsection{Held-out evaluation}
\begin{proposition}[Frozen-policy certificate]
Freeze $K$ policies before $n$ independent problem evaluations. Let $D_{ik}\in[-1,1]$ be the per-problem difference in mean success over a fixed number of seeds from a fixed baseline. With probability at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously for all policies,
\[
\Delta_k\geq\overline D_k-\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)/n}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding for independent observations of range width two gives
$P(\overline D_k-\Delta_k\geq t)\leq\exp(-nt^2/2)$.
Set $t=\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)/n}$ and union-bound over $K$. Within-problem correlation between policies or seeds does not require independence across those observations.
\end{proof}
For deterministic weights $w_i\geq0$ summing to one, weighted Hoeffding replaces the radius by $\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)\sum_iw_i^2}$. At $n=3000,K=1,\alpha=.05$, the unweighted radius is 0.04469. Cost-penalized utility must use its actual bounded range or be rescaled; the binary-success range cannot silently be reused.

Problem-grouped cross-fitting prevents direct training leakage but leaves dependence between fold contributions through overlapping training samples. Fixed-contribution bootstrap intervals have no generic nominal coverage. An exact illustration uses independent $Y_1,Y_2\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(.5)$ and a cross-fitted rule that activates the positive-reward action on each problem only if the other outcome is one. Both held-out contributions equal $Y_1Y_2$. Their mean expectation is .25, but resampling either realized pair $(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$ gives a point interval excluding .25. This counterexample rejects a universal guarantee, not every possible asymptotic procedure.

Adaptive peeking requires an appropriate sequential method; a test-selected prompt or feature schema is test reuse. A binding study-wide query budget can couple task outcomes unless allocation is part of the evaluated protocol. Independent problem sampling is additionally needed to generalize from a fixed checkpoint list to a task population.

\subsection{Opportunity-cost-aware pruning audits}
Fix $N$ logged proposed-pruning states and complete matched-budget keep/reallocate and prune/reallocate protocols. Let $L_i$ be their expected utility difference. Draw audit indicator $R_i$ with known $p_i>0$, independent of future replay outcomes conditional on the logged state. Suppose $\widehat L_i$ is an unbiased estimate of that state's difference.
\begin{proposition}[Audit weighting]
$\widehat L=N^{-1}\sum_iR_i\widehat L_i/p_i$ is unbiased for $N^{-1}\sum_iL_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional independence and unbiasedness give $\mathbb E[R_i\widehat L_i/p_i]=p_iL_i/p_i=L_i$. Sum and divide by $N$.
\end{proof}
Small probabilities can produce severe variance. The target is the logged state distribution, not the updated controller's new distribution. A retained branch's success under free additional compute omits the opportunity cost and estimates a different target. Reusing audits for both controller updates and evaluation is adaptive reuse.

\subsection{Transfer}
For aligned states, actions and utility scales define $r_D(h,\pi)=\max_a Q_D(h,a)-Q_D(h,\pi(h))$. Assume $P_T\ll P_S$, $dP_T/dP_S\leq\kappa$, and $\sup_{h,a}|Q_T(h,a)-Q_S(h,a)|\leq\zeta$ on the relevant support.
\begin{proposition}[Conditional transfer bound]
$\operatorname{Regret}_T(\pi)\leq\kappa\operatorname{Regret}_S(\pi)+2\zeta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The uniform value bound implies $\max_aQ_T(h,a)\leq\max_aQ_S(h,a)+\zeta$ and $Q_T(h,\pi(h))\geq Q_S(h,\pi(h))-\zeta$, hence $r_T\leq r_S+2\zeta$. Integrate under $P_T$ and use nonnegativity of $r_S$ and the density-ratio bound.
\end{proof}
New families may violate overlap; new generators or budgets can make $\zeta$ large. Aligning compressed states adds representation assumptions. No arbitrary-OOD, discovery or alignment guarantee follows.

\subsection{Selective acquisition}
Freeze complete no-query and query-then-control protocols, including failures, latency and budget effects, with potential utilities $U_0,U_J$. A pre-query gate $\eta(W)\in\{0,1\}$ cannot inspect $J$. Put $d(w)=\mathbb E[U_J-U_0\mid W=w]$.
\begin{proposition}[Acquisition value and regret]
\[
V(\eta)=\mathbb E U_0+\mathbb E[\eta(W)d(W)],\qquad
\eta^*(w)=\mathbf1\{d(w)>0\}.
\]
Its regret is $\mathbb E[|d(W)|\mathbf1\{\eta(W)\ne\eta^*(W)\}]$. If $|\widehat d-d|\leq\epsilon$ and $\widehat\eta=\mathbf1\{\widehat d>0\}$, this regret is at most $\epsilon P(|d(W)|\leq\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition the utility $\eta(W)U_J+(1-\eta(W))U_0$ on $W$ to obtain the value identity. Maximizing its integrand gives the sign rule, and each wrong sign loses $|d(w)|$. A wrong estimated sign implies $|d(w)|\leq|\widehat d(w)-d(w)|\leq\epsilon$; integrate.
\end{proof}
The component policies must be frozen before independent acquisition-effect labeling, or fitted through an appropriately nested procedure. Already-paid responses identify information value rather than purchase value. Subtracting average latency from a token-matched run generally cannot reconstruct deadline-constrained outcomes.

\section{Uniform selection in a shared candidate pool}
This secondary analysis was specified during v3 collection without inspecting partial success rates; it does not replace the frozen sampled-uniform baseline. For a fixed checkpoint and candidate pool of size $K$, let $p_j$ be candidate $j$'s continuation success probability and let $\bar Y_j$ average its recorded continuations. A uniformly randomized selector has conditional value
\[
V_{\mathrm{unif}}=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K}p_j,\qquad
\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}}=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\bar Y_j.
\]
Linearity of expectation proves unbiasedness conditional on the checkpoint and pool when each continuation average is unbiased. Independence across candidates is unnecessary. This is an average, not an outcome-informed maximum. For a hypothetical uniform index $U$ independent of the complete recorded outcome table $\mathcal T$, $\mathbb E[\bar Y_U\mid\mathcal T]=\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}}$; the law of total variance gives $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}})\leq\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y_U)$. This is standard conditional averaging, not a new theorem or evidence of semantic benefit. Problem-level clustering remains necessary, and one pool per problem does not identify within-problem variation over newly generated pools. Expected deployment cost charges the full candidate pool plus the average selected-continuation cost; all-arm collection cost is separate.

\section{Binary intervention gates and ties}
For any coupling of binary outcomes $(Y_c,Y_a)$ conditional on cheap features $W=w$, let $p_+=P(Y_a=1,Y_c=0\mid w)$ and $p_-=P(Y_a=0,Y_c=1\mid w)$. Define $r=p_++p_-$ and $\eta=p_+/r$ when $r>0$, with $\eta=.5$ otherwise. Enumerating the four outcome pairs gives
\[
\Delta(w)=\mathbb E[Y_a-Y_c\mid w]=p_+-p_-=r(2\eta-1).
\]
Predicting an outright win and thresholding its probability at .5 need not select positive expected advantage. For a binary gate $g$, the loss
\[
\ell_g=\mathbf1\{Y_a>Y_c\}(1-g)+\mathbf1\{Y_a<Y_c\}g
\]
satisfies $\mathbb E\ell_g=P(Y_a>Y_c)-[V(g)-\mathbb E Y_c]$, by direct expansion. Ties contribute zero. Preserve original problem and full-pair weights when dropping ties; renormalizing each problem by its discordant count changes the target.

For an interior fitted probability $q(w)\in(0,1)$, set $g_q=\mathbf1\{q>.5\}$ and let $g^*$ use $\eta$. The population excess weighted logarithmic loss is
\[
\mathcal E_{\log}=\mathbb E[r(W)\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Bern}(\eta(W))\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(q(W)))].
\]
Then
\[
V(g^*)-V(g_q)\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb E[r(W)]\mathcal E_{\log}}.
\]
To prove it, wrong-sign loss is $r|2\eta-1|\leq2r|\eta-q|$. For fixed $q$, the function $\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Bern}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(q))-2(p-q)^2$ has nonnegative second derivative $1/[p(1-p)]-4$, and value and derivative zero at $p=q$. Therefore KL is at least $2(p-q)^2$; Cauchy--Schwarz completes the bound. Endpoint predictions require limiting care and may have infinite loss. This statement concerns population unregularized excess loss, not training accuracy or the optimizer's penalized objective. Comparing separate gates' conditional-win probabilities cannot rank several actions because their discordance factors differ.

\section{Theory validation and empirical boundaries}
The repository records fourteen finite checks, including 200 finite-MDP performance-difference checks, 500 greedy-regret constructions, exact acquisition-value examples, adaptive-stopping bias, cross-fit dependence and noisy-oracle optimism. These support implementation consistency, not universal proofs or real Jev efficacy. The reviewed theory notes and baseline appendix supply the provenance for this manuscript integration. None of the untested sequential, transfer or pruning assumptions has been established experimentally.

\subsection{Timeout-completion sensitivity}
A per-call timeout changes the operational policy and is not merely missing data. Let $Y_a$ be its observed binary outcome and $T_a$ indicate any timeout in the episode. Consider a hypothetical completion regime with outcome $Y_a^\dagger$, and explicitly assume $Y_a^\dagger=Y_a$ when $T_a=0$. Without assumptions about timed-out episodes, define $m_a=\mathbb E[(1-T_a)Y_a]$ and $q_a=P(T_a=1)$. Since the unobserved contribution lies between zero and $q_a$,
\[
m_a\leq\mathbb E Y_a^\dagger\leq m_a+q_a,
\qquad
m_a-m_b-q_b\leq\mathbb E[Y_a^\dagger-Y_b^\dagger]\leq m_a+q_a-m_b.
\]
This elementary worst-case bound requires no independent-censoring assumption. Plugging in empirical fractions gives a finite-sample completion envelope, not a population confidence interval; sampling uncertainty remains. The invariance assumption need not hold after changing hardware, sampling, resource competition or the whole controller. Observed success under the actual frozen timeout policy remains the measured endpoint. This sensitivity analysis cannot repair unequal resource contracts or establish a timeout-free effect.

\section{CUDA and published-baseline qualification}
A separate pinned BF16 Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 run completed on an H200 with exact greedy prefix-resume agreement, an 8k-context check and batched generation. The saved-record audit passed. Peak allocated GPU memory was 9.31 GiB; batch throughput was 5.78 emitted tokens/s. These measurements establish backend compatibility, not a speedup or reasoning benefit. The subsequent two-problem continue/segmented-sham/GUARD-adaptation runtime check completed in 14.5 allocated GPU-minutes. Its independent audit reconciled six outcomes and 75 calls, including two natural branch triggers. All three policies solved the same one of two tasks; this establishes no policy benefit. The fresh twelve-problem H100 development comparison completed in 23 minutes 56 seconds: continue solved 5/12, segmented sham and GUARD each solved 6/12. All 430 calls and 36 labels passed audit; only three natural branching events occurred. This does not establish an advantage over sham. The cost audit also found one continuation-call timeout versus none for the segmented policies; unequal exposure to the per-call deadline limits interpretation of the continuation comparison. GUARD and sham processed 227,210 and 209,329 prompt tokens respectively. The fresh randomized-timing comparison, with opportunity probability fixed at 3/55, completed on twelve tasks: GUARD, randomized branching and sham each solved 8/12, whereas continuation solved 10/12 with one timeout. GUARD and random disagreed on two tasks, giving a descriptive paired range of $-25$ to $+25$ percentage points. There were two triggers per branching policy; all 567 calls and 48 outcomes passed audit. The fixed 96-problem B200 development study completed in 1 hour 33 minutes 37 seconds: continue solved 54/96, sham 66/96, GUARD 61/96 and random 59/96. All 4,583 calls and 384 outcomes passed audit. GUARD-minus-random was +2.08 points (descriptive range $-6.25$ to $+10.42$), and GUARD-minus-sham was $-5.21$ points (range $-12.5$ to $+2.08$). GUARD triggered 37 times versus random 14, demonstrating that frozen opportunity probability did not match realized intervention counts. These results do not justify a positive branching claim. The fresh local repair replication recorded 480 outcomes over 24 problems with four repeats: continue 63/96, sham 68/96, suffix repair 57/96, recheck 56/96 and segment repair 54/96. All 631 calls passed the record audit. Segment repair minus sham was $-14.58$ points (descriptive range $-27.08$ to $-2.08$). The final segment-repair episode timed out at the run bound; the supervisor receipt did not finalize, so no verified process exit code is claimed. Two disjoint-repeat diagnostics modestly exceeded continue but did not establish improvement over best static sham. The subsequent 48-problem static-sham replication completed: sham solved 47/96 and continuation 46/96, with a paired difference of +1.04 percentage points (descriptive problem-bootstrap range $-7.29$ to $+9.38$); no reliable interruption benefit was established. An exploratory leave-one-problem-out TF-IDF/Ridge controller (fixed regularization, vocabulary fitted within each training fold) selected sham in all 24 held-out folds and matched the training-selected static policy. Thus its apparent gain over continue supplies no evidence of individualized control. Overlapping training folds preclude interpreting its descriptive bootstrap as an independent prospective confidence guarantee. BF16 outcomes are not pooled with MLX 4-bit evidence.

The separate InterWhen K-stable Game24 adaptation was a published-baseline qualification, not an InterWhen reproduction: it used our cached Qwen3-4B checkpoint, 96 fixed rows from the public training split, one seed, and an independent exact AST/Fraction outcome checker. The complete-record audit passed all 192 episodes and 206 model calls; there were no timeouts and 14 natural monitor triggers. The adaptation solved 54/96 tasks versus 60/96 for continuation, a paired difference of $-6.25$ percentage points (6 wins, 12 losses, 78 ties; descriptive task-bootstrap 95\% interval $[-14.58,+2.08]$). It generated an average 40.20 more output tokens per task than continuation; mean model service time was 1.25 seconds lower, but the generation cost did not decrease. The job used one B200 for 54 minutes 14 seconds (0.904 GPU-hours). This public-training-split result is descriptive, not confirmatory, and does not establish that the original method is ineffective; it does show that this specific adaptation produced no savings or accuracy benefit under this protocol.

An independent rerun of the local action-replication record and outcome auditors reconciled all 480 planned episodes across 24 development problems (two task families), 631 durable calls, and zero label disagreements. The original process receipt remains stale and its recorded PID is absent, so no worker exit code is inferred. Continue solved 63/96, equal-token sham 68/96, suffix repair 57/96, recheck 56/96, and segment repair 54/96. The problem-weighted segment-minus-sham difference was $-14.58$ percentage points (descriptive cluster-bootstrap interval $[-27.08,-2.08]$); recheck-minus-sham was $-12.50$ points (interval $[-21.88,-4.17]$). Segment repair generated a mean 1,440 tokens per episode versus 1,395 for sham and used 36.32 versus 35.06 seconds of model-service time; these are total-episode costs, including discarded work. This small, exploratory, multiply compared development sample is conditional on eligible checkpoints and cannot establish general repair harm. It does not justify scaling these unchanged repair actions or support a confirmatory claim.
