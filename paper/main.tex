\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether typed semantic judgments add decision value when selecting interventions in a frozen language model's observable reasoning. We separate judge quality, action effectiveness, policy learning, and acquisition cost. Initial local experiments used independently verified procedural graph and arithmetic tasks. A 24-problem mechanism screen and a separate 24-problem prospective diagnostic did not demonstrate an incremental benefit from Jev features. These small experiments concern narrow interventions and do not establish general ineffectiveness. An ongoing curriculum evaluates intervention quality and stronger baselines before larger, sequential or transfer studies. We present explicit estimands and reproducibility boundaries rather than claim a new control principle or a positive result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question and prior art}
The question is whether inexpensive semantic features improve the selection of useful interventions enough to justify acquisition costs. It is not whether a judge agrees with a final verifier. Process-advantage verification already relates reasoning steps to subsequent success \cite{pav}; reward-guided speculative decoding controls draft acceptance \cite{rsd}. Work on intervention value and learned gates \cite{calibration,dial} limits novelty claims for checkpoint branching and outcome-grounded control. GUARD provides a relevant local-branching precedent \cite{guard}. Our contribution remains under investigation; substituting Jev for a reward model is not by itself a new methodological principle.

\section{Estimand and experimental separation}
Let $H$ be a complete observable checkpoint, $b$ the remaining resource allowance, $a$ an intervention and $\mu$ a frozen subsequent policy. For independently verified terminal success $Y$ and a specified cost $C$, define
\[
Q^\mu(h,b,a)=\mathbb E[Y^{a,\mu}-\lambda C^{a,\mu}\mid H=h,b],\qquad
\Delta^\mu(h,b,a)=Q^\mu(h,b,a)-Q^\mu(h,b,a_0).
\]
Cost units and $\lambda$ must be declared. We currently report success, tokens, service time and dollars separately; there is no measured hardware-independent cost conversion. All-arm experiments clone the same observable checkpoint and run actual continuations. Gold task data is used by the terminal evaluator, never by the intervention selector. Multiple checkpoints or repeats from one problem do not create independent task samples.

We distinguish (i) whether the available actions have useful, reproducible differences; (ii) whether semantic features predict those differences beyond cheap text and token statistics; and (iii) whether the resulting frozen policy improves fresh complete episodes. A failure at one layer does not identify all other layers as ineffective.

\section{Executed initial experiments}
The generator was frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 under local MLX four-bit inference. This is a non-thinking instruction model: interventions operate on generated explanations, not inaccessible internal reasoning. The tasks were procedural weighted directed paths and arithmetic constructions, checked by independent executable validators. These are instrumentation and development domains, not evidence of scientific discovery.

The mechanism screen enrolled 24 fresh problems, with three actions and four continuations per action. An online newline checkpoint was targeted at 256 emitted tokens, capped at 384. The total generated-token allowance was 1,024 per hypothetical episode, including the initial prefix and discarded work. Repair rewound at most 128 tokens; branch generated two candidates of at most 64 tokens and selected by mean base likelihood. These are deliberately narrow action definitions.

\section{Costs, integrity and limitations}
The mechanism run recorded 201,006 generated tokens and 4,899.32 model-service seconds, excluding its separately recorded local-feature acquisition. Prospective collection used 36,762 generated tokens and 883.16 model-service seconds. Shared collection cost differs from each policy's hypothetical deployment cost. Local judge acquisition added a mean 68.46 tokens and 2.146 seconds per prospective problem; Jev added a mean 0.215 seconds. Matched generator-token caps are not matched total compute or latency.

Independent audits reconstructed recorded token/call chains, interventions and final labels, finding no outcome disagreements. These checks are not fresh inference replay or externally attested timestamps. Public terminal tasks, texts, labels and costs permit model-free outcome reproduction; hosted features and a private fitted controller are excluded, limiting public feature-level reproducibility. No broad OOD, long-horizon, alignment or innovation claim is supported by these initial results.

\section{Ongoing curriculum and missing evidence}
A new development run compares continue, a neutral prompt, suffix repair, rechecking without deletion, and complete-segment reconstruction at a 2,048-token allowance. It completed eight fresh development problems with two continuations per action (80 outcomes). Continue, sham, suffix repair, recheck and full-segment reconstruction solved 11/16, 10/16, 11/16, 11/16 and 12/16 respectively. All 104 recorded calls and terminal labels passed the independent audit. The segment-minus-continue difference was +6.25 percentage points, with a descriptive problem-stratified range of $-18.75$ to $+31.25$ points. This small exploratory difference does not establish benefit. Four problems per family are insufficient to establish stable heterogeneity; historical v1 is not a randomized budget comparator. A separate eight-problem branch qualification used a common three-candidate pool and two continuations per candidate. Continue, uniform selection, likelihood, entropy ranking and local semantic selection solved 9/16, 10/16, 11/16, 11/16 and 11/16 respectively; all 126 calls and 64 distinct outcomes passed audit. The informed-selector minus continue difference was descriptively +12.5 points (problem-stratified bootstrap range 0 to 25 points), with no aggregate semantic increment over cheap ranking. There were no Jev calls. This small development result motivates fresh replication, not efficacy claims. The fresh 24-problem, four-repeat replication completed with 384 unique outcomes and 677 audited calls. Continue, uniform, likelihood, entropy, local semantic and Jev semantic selection solved 48/96, 44/96, 43/96, 44/96, 42/96 and 42/96 respectively. Jev-minus-likelihood was $-1.04$ percentage points (descriptive problem-bootstrap range $-5.21$ to $+3.13$); Jev-minus-continue was $-6.25$ points (range $-14.58$ to $+2.08$). Identical aggregate local/Jev outcomes do not establish equivalence. Neither independent two-repeat selection/evaluation split showed an outcome-informed candidate-selection gain over sampled uniform. This is development evidence against scaling this particular fixed branch-selector design unchanged, not proof that intervention control or Jev is generally ineffective. Static, randomized/rate-matched, strong cheap-text, local-judge and compatible published-controller comparisons remain required for later claims. A component inspired by a published method is explicitly an adaptation, not a reproduction. A segment-boundary GUARD adaptation now has CPU contract tests for intervention timing and discarded-work accounting, and a frozen four-task runtime protocol with an explicit segmented sham control, with local MLX runtime qualification completed: all three policies solved 3/4 tasks, with 153 audited calls and three natural triggers. The separately audited CUDA instrumentation result is reported below.

Larger independent evaluation, sequential control, horizon and generator transfer, and controller-guided training are future gated experiments. Their absence cannot be replaced by hypothetical results or theorem assumptions. A failed setting motivates diagnosis or a justified redesign; it does not close the original scientific question.

\section{Theory, assumptions and scope}
These propositions specialize established experimental identification, decision theory and policy learning. They are not claimed as new general theorems. Their conditions are not certified by a judge's confidence or by the present small experiments. The observable history includes exact tokens and formatting, candidate frontier, tool/environment state, remaining resources, and the fixed model, quantization, sampler and backend. Changing any of these may change the estimand. Random continuations define distributions of potential outcomes, not a uniquely observable individual counterfactual future.

\subsection{Identification and eligibility}
\begin{proposition}[Checkpoint identification]
Suppose interventions and the subsequent policy $\mu$ are well defined; replay restores the checkpoint and environment without cross-arm interference; assignment is independent of potential outcomes conditional on $H$; each studied action has positive assignment probability; and failures/truncations follow a prespecified inclusion rule. Then
\[
Q^\mu(h,a)=\mathbb E[U\mid H=h,A=a].
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consistency gives $U=U^{a,\mu}$ on $A=a$. Conditional randomization implies
$\mathbb E[U^{a,\mu}\mid H=h,A=a]=\mathbb E[U^{a,\mu}\mid H=h]$.
Positivity makes the former conditional mean observable, yielding the result.
\end{proof}
For complete all-arm collection, the mean of $m_{ia}$ replays is conditionally unbiased if its positive replicate count is fixed independently of their outcomes and every replay has the correct marginal continuation distribution. Independence between replays is needed for common elementary variance formulas, not for linearity of expectation. Outcome-dependent stopping can bias the stopped mean. Equal integer seeds after diverging prefixes do not automatically give useful common-random-number coupling.

Let $R$ be a frozen checkpoint-construction protocol and $E$ its pre-intervention eligibility event. The conditional estimand is on $P_R(H\mid E=1)$. If two policies use the same $R$, intervene once and have identical ineligible fallback, then
\[
V_R(\pi)-V_R(\pi_0)=P_R(E=1)\mathbb E[Q_R^\mu(H,\pi(H))-Q_R^\mu(H,\pi_0(H))\mid E=1].
\]
To prove this, decompose the full-episode difference by $E$, cancel the common fallback term and apply the conditional action means. Different prefix builders, eligibility rules or repeated interventions invalidate that derivation. A generate-then-rewind prefix selected using later emitted text is not an online stopping-time checkpoint, even if its retained tokens exactly match a natural prefix. Historical constructed-state experiments must remain separately labeled.

For sparse randomized arms with known $e_a(H)>0$, a frozen policy $\pi$ and independently fitted outcome functions $m_a$, define
\[
\phi_\pi=m_{\pi(H)}(H)+\frac{\mathbf1\{A=\pi(H)\}}{e_A(H)}[U-m_A(H)].
\]
Conditional on $H$ and the fitted functions, the correction has expectation $Q^\mu(H,\pi(H))-m_{\pi(H)}(H)$, so $\mathbb E\phi_\pi=V^\mu(\pi)$. Estimated propensities require additional consistency assumptions; the label ``doubly robust'' alone supplies no guarantee. Cross-fitting must separate original problems.

\subsection{Semantic representation and acquisition}
\begin{proposition}[Free-information comparison]
For a fixed distribution of potential utilities, fixed actions and nested representations $W$ and $(W,J)$, define
\[
V^*_{W,J}=\mathbb E\max_a\mathbb E[U^a\mid W,J],\qquad
V^*_W=\mathbb E\max_a\mathbb E[U^a\mid W].
\]
If acquiring $J$ is free and changes neither state nor budget, $V^*_{W,J}\geq V^*_W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every policy measurable in $W$ is also admissible with $(W,J)$ and can ignore $J$. Taking a supremum over the larger policy class cannot decrease value.
\end{proof}
Finite fitted learners need not improve. If one action is optimal everywhere, informative semantic classifications can have no decision value. If $J=f(H)$, or its randomness is independent of rollout outcomes conditional on $H$, it contributes no external information beyond full history; the hypothesis concerns a computationally useful representation relative to feasible cheap alternatives.

For deterministic known acquisition cost $c_J$, with acquisition affecting subsequent behavior only through information and remaining resource vector $B$, the pre-query comparison is
\[
\mathbb E_{J\mid w,B}\!\left[\max_{a\in\mathcal A(B-c_J)}Q(w,J,B-c_J,a)\right]
\quad\hbox{versus}\quad
\max_{a\in\mathcal A(B)}Q_0(w,B,a).
\]
The maximum belongs inside the expectation over the unobserved signal. Stochastic failure, latency or usage requires integrating over the entire acquisition outcome, including fallback, exhaustion and any changed external state. Hard constraints and cost preferences may coexist, but their units must be declared and fees must not accidentally be subtracted twice.

\subsection{Action-value error and replay precision}
\begin{proposition}[Greedy regret]
At a checkpoint, suppose $\max_a|\widehat Q(a)-Q(a)|\leq\epsilon$. If $a^*\in\arg\max_a Q(a)$ and $\widehat a\in\arg\max_a\widehat Q(a)$, then $Q(a^*)-Q(\widehat a)\leq2\epsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert $\widehat Q(a^*)$ and $\widehat Q(\widehat a)$ between the two true values. The estimated-value difference is nonpositive by selection, while each of the two remaining errors is at most $\epsilon$.
\end{proof}
A unique optimal action with gap exceeding $2\epsilon$ must be selected. Representation and estimation errors add by the triangle inequality when both are bounded uniformly at the relevant states. Neither calibration nor AUROC establishes such a bound.

For $M$ fixed checkpoints, $K$ actions and $m$ independent Bernoulli replays per pair, Hoeffding and a union bound yield
\[
P\!\left(\max_{i,a}|\widehat Q_i(a)-Q_i(a)|>\epsilon\right)
\leq2MK\exp(-2m\epsilon^2).
\]
Thus $m\geq\log(2MK/\delta)/(2\epsilon^2)$ suffices. At $M=48,K=3,\delta=.05,\epsilon=.10$, the rounded requirement is 433 replays per pair. A few repeats can support pooled modeling; they do not supply reliable individual best-action labels. A sample maximum evaluated on its own selection data is optimistic. Independent continuations must assess the selected rule.

\subsection{Sequential control}
Use a finite-horizon history-state process with resource counters, time and acquisition phase included in $H_t$. Absorb early termination with zero future reward and use zero terminal value. Let the deterministic baseline choose $a_{0t}(h)$, with
\[
Q_t^\mu(h,a)=\mathbb E[r_t+V_{t+1}^\mu(H_{t+1})\mid h,a],\quad
A_t^\mu(h,a)=Q_t^\mu(h,a)-V_t^\mu(h).
\]
\begin{proposition}[Conditional sequential improvement]
Suppose both policies share initial distribution, transitions, reward and resource envelope. On every history reachable by $\pi$, assume all errors satisfy
$|\widehat Q_t(h,a)-Q_t^\mu(h,a)|\leq\epsilon_t(h)$.
If $\pi$ deviates only when
\[
\widehat Q_t(h,a)-\widehat Q_t(h,a_{0t})>2\epsilon_t(h),
\]
then $V^\pi\geq V^\mu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a switched state the true difference is at least the estimated difference minus $2\epsilon_t(h)$, hence positive. At an unswitched state it is zero. Since $V_t^\mu(h)=Q_t^\mu(h,a_{0t})$, all visited advantages are nonnegative. Conditional expectation and telescoping give
\[
\mathbb E_\pi\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}A_t^\mu(H_t,A_t)
=\mathbb E_\pi\sum_t[r_t+V_{t+1}^\mu(H_{t+1})-V_t^\mu(H_t)]
=V^\pi-V^\mu\geq0.
\]
\end{proof}
The uniform premise covers newly reached states, not only baseline prefixes. One-step replay therefore does not establish this result's premises in deployment. A gate evaluated after paying for a judge can only compare policies in that post-query state; query purchase must separately have nonnegative advantage or be included in the full intervention. Judge confidence is not the required simultaneous error bound.

\subsection{Held-out evaluation}
\begin{proposition}[Frozen-policy certificate]
Freeze $K$ policies before $n$ independent problem evaluations. Let $D_{ik}\in[-1,1]$ be the per-problem difference in mean success over a fixed number of seeds from a fixed baseline. With probability at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously for all policies,
\[
\Delta_k\geq\overline D_k-\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)/n}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding for independent observations of range width two gives
$P(\overline D_k-\Delta_k\geq t)\leq\exp(-nt^2/2)$.
Set $t=\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)/n}$ and union-bound over $K$. Within-problem correlation between policies or seeds does not require independence across those observations.
\end{proof}
For deterministic weights $w_i\geq0$ summing to one, weighted Hoeffding replaces the radius by $\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)\sum_iw_i^2}$. At $n=3000,K=1,\alpha=.05$, the unweighted radius is 0.04469. Cost-penalized utility must use its actual bounded range or be rescaled; the binary-success range cannot silently be reused.

Problem-grouped cross-fitting prevents direct training leakage but leaves dependence between fold contributions through overlapping training samples. Fixed-contribution bootstrap intervals have no generic nominal coverage. An exact illustration uses independent $Y_1,Y_2\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(.5)$ and a cross-fitted rule that activates the positive-reward action on each problem only if the other outcome is one. Both held-out contributions equal $Y_1Y_2$. Their mean expectation is .25, but resampling either realized pair $(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$ gives a point interval excluding .25. This counterexample rejects a universal guarantee, not every possible asymptotic procedure.

Adaptive peeking requires an appropriate sequential method; a test-selected prompt or feature schema is test reuse. A binding study-wide query budget can couple task outcomes unless allocation is part of the evaluated protocol. Independent problem sampling is additionally needed to generalize from a fixed checkpoint list to a task population.

\subsection{Opportunity-cost-aware pruning audits}
Fix $N$ logged proposed-pruning states and complete matched-budget keep/reallocate and prune/reallocate protocols. Let $L_i$ be their expected utility difference. Draw audit indicator $R_i$ with known $p_i>0$, independent of future replay outcomes conditional on the logged state. Suppose $\widehat L_i$ is an unbiased estimate of that state's difference.
\begin{proposition}[Audit weighting]
$\widehat L=N^{-1}\sum_iR_i\widehat L_i/p_i$ is unbiased for $N^{-1}\sum_iL_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional independence and unbiasedness give $\mathbb E[R_i\widehat L_i/p_i]=p_iL_i/p_i=L_i$. Sum and divide by $N$.
\end{proof}
Small probabilities can produce severe variance. The target is the logged state distribution, not the updated controller's new distribution. A retained branch's success under free additional compute omits the opportunity cost and estimates a different target. Reusing audits for both controller updates and evaluation is adaptive reuse.

\subsection{Transfer}
For aligned states, actions and utility scales define $r_D(h,\pi)=\max_a Q_D(h,a)-Q_D(h,\pi(h))$. Assume $P_T\ll P_S$, $dP_T/dP_S\leq\kappa$, and $\sup_{h,a}|Q_T(h,a)-Q_S(h,a)|\leq\zeta$ on the relevant support.
\begin{proposition}[Conditional transfer bound]
$\operatorname{Regret}_T(\pi)\leq\kappa\operatorname{Regret}_S(\pi)+2\zeta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The uniform value bound implies $\max_aQ_T(h,a)\leq\max_aQ_S(h,a)+\zeta$ and $Q_T(h,\pi(h))\geq Q_S(h,\pi(h))-\zeta$, hence $r_T\leq r_S+2\zeta$. Integrate under $P_T$ and use nonnegativity of $r_S$ and the density-ratio bound.
\end{proof}
New families may violate overlap; new generators or budgets can make $\zeta$ large. Aligning compressed states adds representation assumptions. No arbitrary-OOD, discovery or alignment guarantee follows.

\subsection{Selective acquisition}
Freeze complete no-query and query-then-control protocols, including failures, latency and budget effects, with potential utilities $U_0,U_J$. A pre-query gate $\eta(W)\in\{0,1\}$ cannot inspect $J$. Put $d(w)=\mathbb E[U_J-U_0\mid W=w]$.
\begin{proposition}[Acquisition value and regret]
\[
V(\eta)=\mathbb E U_0+\mathbb E[\eta(W)d(W)],\qquad
\eta^*(w)=\mathbf1\{d(w)>0\}.
\]
Its regret is $\mathbb E[|d(W)|\mathbf1\{\eta(W)\ne\eta^*(W)\}]$. If $|\widehat d-d|\leq\epsilon$ and $\widehat\eta=\mathbf1\{\widehat d>0\}$, this regret is at most $\epsilon P(|d(W)|\leq\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition the utility $\eta(W)U_J+(1-\eta(W))U_0$ on $W$ to obtain the value identity. Maximizing its integrand gives the sign rule, and each wrong sign loses $|d(w)|$. A wrong estimated sign implies $|d(w)|\leq|\widehat d(w)-d(w)|\leq\epsilon$; integrate.
\end{proof}
The component policies must be frozen before independent acquisition-effect labeling, or fitted through an appropriately nested procedure. Already-paid responses identify information value rather than purchase value. Subtracting average latency from a token-matched run generally cannot reconstruct deadline-constrained outcomes.

\section{Uniform selection in a shared candidate pool}
This secondary analysis was specified during v3 collection without inspecting partial success rates; it does not replace the frozen sampled-uniform baseline. For a fixed checkpoint and candidate pool of size $K$, let $p_j$ be candidate $j$'s continuation success probability and let $\bar Y_j$ average its recorded continuations. A uniformly randomized selector has conditional value
\[
V_{\mathrm{unif}}=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K}p_j,\qquad
\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}}=K^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\bar Y_j.
\]
Linearity of expectation proves unbiasedness conditional on the checkpoint and pool when each continuation average is unbiased. Independence across candidates is unnecessary. This is an average, not an outcome-informed maximum. For a hypothetical uniform index $U$ independent of the complete recorded outcome table $\mathcal T$, $\mathbb E[\bar Y_U\mid\mathcal T]=\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}}$; the law of total variance gives $\operatorname{Var}(\widehat V_{\mathrm{unif}})\leq\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y_U)$. This is standard conditional averaging, not a new theorem or evidence of semantic benefit. Problem-level clustering remains necessary, and one pool per problem does not identify within-problem variation over newly generated pools. Expected deployment cost charges the full candidate pool plus the average selected-continuation cost; all-arm collection cost is separate.

\section{Binary intervention gates and ties}
For any coupling of binary outcomes $(Y_c,Y_a)$ conditional on cheap features $W=w$, let $p_+=P(Y_a=1,Y_c=0\mid w)$ and $p_-=P(Y_a=0,Y_c=1\mid w)$. Define $r=p_++p_-$ and $\eta=p_+/r$ when $r>0$, with $\eta=.5$ otherwise. Enumerating the four outcome pairs gives
\[
\Delta(w)=\mathbb E[Y_a-Y_c\mid w]=p_+-p_-=r(2\eta-1).
\]
Predicting an outright win and thresholding its probability at .5 need not select positive expected advantage. For a binary gate $g$, the loss
\[
\ell_g=\mathbf1\{Y_a>Y_c\}(1-g)+\mathbf1\{Y_a<Y_c\}g
\]
satisfies $\mathbb E\ell_g=P(Y_a>Y_c)-[V(g)-\mathbb E Y_c]$, by direct expansion. Ties contribute zero. Preserve original problem and full-pair weights when dropping ties; renormalizing each problem by its discordant count changes the target.

For an interior fitted probability $q(w)\in(0,1)$, set $g_q=\mathbf1\{q>.5\}$ and let $g^*$ use $\eta$. The population excess weighted logarithmic loss is
\[
\mathcal E_{\log}=\mathbb E[r(W)\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Bern}(\eta(W))\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(q(W)))].
\]
Then
\[
V(g^*)-V(g_q)\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb E[r(W)]\mathcal E_{\log}}.
\]
To prove it, wrong-sign loss is $r|2\eta-1|\leq2r|\eta-q|$. For fixed $q$, the function $\operatorname{KL}(\operatorname{Bern}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Bern}(q))-2(p-q)^2$ has nonnegative second derivative $1/[p(1-p)]-4$, and value and derivative zero at $p=q$. Therefore KL is at least $2(p-q)^2$; Cauchy--Schwarz completes the bound. Endpoint predictions require limiting care and may have infinite loss. This statement concerns population unregularized excess loss, not training accuracy or the optimizer's penalized objective. Comparing separate gates' conditional-win probabilities cannot rank several actions because their discordance factors differ.

\section{Theory validation and empirical boundaries}
The repository records fourteen finite checks, including 200 finite-MDP performance-difference checks, 500 greedy-regret constructions, exact acquisition-value examples, adaptive-stopping bias, cross-fit dependence and noisy-oracle optimism. These support implementation consistency, not universal proofs or real Jev efficacy. The reviewed theory notes and baseline appendix supply the provenance for this manuscript integration. None of the untested sequential, transfer or pruning assumptions has been established experimentally.

\subsection{Timeout-completion sensitivity}
A per-call timeout changes the operational policy and is not merely missing data. Let $Y_a$ be its observed binary outcome and $T_a$ indicate any timeout in the episode. Consider a hypothetical completion regime with outcome $Y_a^\dagger$, and explicitly assume $Y_a^\dagger=Y_a$ when $T_a=0$. Without assumptions about timed-out episodes, define $m_a=\mathbb E[(1-T_a)Y_a]$ and $q_a=P(T_a=1)$. Since the unobserved contribution lies between zero and $q_a$,
\[
m_a\leq\mathbb E Y_a^\dagger\leq m_a+q_a,
\qquad
m_a-m_b-q_b\leq\mathbb E[Y_a^\dagger-Y_b^\dagger]\leq m_a+q_a-m_b.
\]
This elementary worst-case bound requires no independent-censoring assumption. Plugging in empirical fractions gives a finite-sample completion envelope, not a population confidence interval; sampling uncertainty remains. The invariance assumption need not hold after changing hardware, sampling, resource competition or the whole controller. Observed success under the actual frozen timeout policy remains the measured endpoint. This sensitivity analysis cannot repair unequal resource contracts or establish a timeout-free effect.

\section{CUDA infrastructure qualification}
A separate pinned BF16 Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 run completed on an H200 with exact greedy prefix-resume agreement, an 8k-context check and batched generation. The saved-record audit passed. Peak allocated GPU memory was 9.31 GiB; batch throughput was 5.78 emitted tokens/s. These measurements establish backend compatibility, not a speedup or reasoning benefit. The subsequent two-problem continue/segmented-sham/GUARD-adaptation runtime check completed in 14.5 allocated GPU-minutes. Its independent audit reconciled six outcomes and 75 calls, including two natural branch triggers. All three policies solved the same one of two tasks; this establishes no policy benefit. The fresh twelve-problem H100 development comparison completed in 23 minutes 56 seconds: continue solved 5/12, segmented sham and GUARD each solved 6/12. All 430 calls and 36 labels passed audit; only three natural branching events occurred. This does not establish an advantage over sham. The cost audit also found one continuation-call timeout versus none for the segmented policies; unequal exposure to the per-call deadline limits interpretation of the continuation comparison. GUARD and sham processed 227,210 and 209,329 prompt tokens respectively. The fresh randomized-timing comparison, with opportunity probability fixed at 3/55, completed on twelve tasks: GUARD, randomized branching and sham each solved 8/12, whereas continuation solved 10/12 with one timeout. GUARD and random disagreed on two tasks, giving a descriptive paired range of $-25$ to $+25$ percentage points. There were two triggers per branching policy; all 567 calls and 48 outcomes passed audit. A fixed 96-problem development precision study is submitted; it is not powered to establish a three-point effect and its outcomes are pending. BF16 outcomes are not pooled with MLX 4-bit evidence.
